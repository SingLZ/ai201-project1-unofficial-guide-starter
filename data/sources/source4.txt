Title: Any apartment complexes that don’t suck?
URL: https://www.reddit.com/r/SanJose/comments/zfdbmt/any_apartment_complexes_that_dont_suck/
Type: Reddit thread

Original Post:
The poster asks whether any San Jose apartment complexes are decent. They are open to anywhere in the San Jose area, have a maximum monthly budget of $4,600, and need a place that allows cats. Their two must-haves are secure parking and no insect infestations.

[COMMENT]
A commenter said they refuse to pay more than $2,000 for a one-bedroom apartment and questioned high rents for small spaces.

[COMMENT]
A commenter said apartment reviews tend to skew negative because unhappy residents are more motivated to leave reviews. They said their complex went downhill after the pandemic and they planned to leave a negative review to warn others.

[COMMENT]
A commenter advised avoiding Essex properties, saying centralized operations after the pandemic were linked to more crime and slow repairs.

[COMMENT]
A commenter said they lived at Avalon Campbell. They said it is gated, close to the train and downtown Campbell, has access to the farmers market and restaurants, and had no problem with a cat.

[COMMENT]
The same commenter said they also lived in The Woods, which is a much bigger community that is still gated and sometimes included garages. They said the location for The Woods was less ideal because there is not much nearby and no train to downtown.

[COMMENT]
A commenter said they lived at Avalon Campbell for a long time and loved it except for the high rent. They liked the gym, pool, trail access, and ability to walk downtown.

[COMMENT]
A commenter mentioned 300 Railway in Campbell as a nice-looking complex near the trail, but said rents were steep when they visited the rental office.

[COMMENT]
A commenter said they lived at 300 Railway for a little over a year. They described the complex as very nice with good amenities and a cool Campbell area, but said their work van was stolen from the parking lot, showing that crime can happen even in better locations.

[COMMENT]
A commenter said they enjoyed staying at The Standard near Willow Glen and had no real complaints. They estimated a one-bedroom around $2,300–$2,400 at the time of the comment.

[COMMENT]
A commenter said Los Gatos Creek Trail being in the backyard is a major plus for some Campbell-area apartments.

[COMMENT]
A commenter recommended renting directly from an owner, such as a condo or house, instead of a mass apartment complex. They said corporate complexes caused many issues, while a good private owner can have more personal incentive to keep the place livable.

[COMMENT]
Another commenter warned that private owners can also be hit or miss. A bad private owner may be worse than a corporate complex because they may ignore maintenance or legal obligations.

[COMMENT]
A commenter said Waterford Place was mostly fine before COVID. They also lived in The Woods for one year and did not have many issues, but disliked living there. They said The Woods is “gated,” but people can still walk in and there can be long lines getting into the complex.

[COMMENT]
A commenter said Riverview had safe gated parking and plenty of covered visitor parking. They reported some break-ins, including a truck with an unlocked cab window, but said the overall parking situation was better than other complexes.

[COMMENT]
A commenter said Riverview had some upstairs-neighbor noise issues, but management allowed them to transfer to another unit. They said the complex was generally no worse than other apartment complexes.

[COMMENT]
A commenter said Miro in downtown San Jose was the best apartment they had stayed at. They liked the high-rise format, parking on the first four floors, amenities, and staff.

[COMMENT]
A commenter asked about Miro parking costs. Another reply said parking was around $275, or $350 for tandem parking, at the time of the thread.
